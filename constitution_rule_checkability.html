Rule Checkability Ledger

Every rule ID that has ever existed across all eight versions of CONSTITUTION.md plus the four _v0* side files — 49 IDs. For each: can a breach even be detected from what the pipeline stores, how would I detect it, and how often did it happen across the 55 paper edits.

14Decidable — a structural test on the beat JSON settles it, no judgement needed.
5Partial — testable in one direction only, or via a proxy, or only where side data exists.
19Not checkable — needs the footage, the full transcript, or an editorial judgement about content.
11N/A — stripped before the producer ever sees it, empty, or with no representation in the output schema.

Three things the grading exposes

Just over a third of the constitution is enforceable. 19 of 49 IDs can be tested at all, and 5 of those only partially. The rest are either editorial judgement (§3, §4, most of §6, §9, §10) or were never visible to the producer in the first place.

11 IDs are dead weight. R1.1–R1.5 and R2.3–R2.6 carry [FOOTAGE] and R3.1 carries [IGNORE], so constitution_view.py strips all ten before the producer's prompt is built. R8.1 governs captions, and there is no caption field in the beat schema. They have never constrained anything.

R1.6 and R1.9 cannot both be satisfied. R1.6 requires the cut to land outside the beat, before its start and after its end. R1.9 forbids same-camera beats overlapping in time, and the fix the agent reaches for is to trim one beat's boundary to the other's — which puts the cut exactly on the join, with no handle either side. 5 of the 7 zero-handle joins in the set were produced by an explicitly R1.9-cited trim. The constitution is asking for two incompatible things and the agent has been resolving it in R1.9's favour every time.

One rule is unbreakable by construction. R1.7 grants a permission and forbids nothing, so no state of the data can violate it.

Scope. 55 paper edits and drafts with surviving JSON, 08-08 to 08-30 2026. Each scored against the constitution version live on its file mtime. Rules are only scored for the window in which their current text applied — R5.1 from 08-13, R5.3's coverage clause and R5.5 from 08-27, R6.8 from 08-26, R5.4 up to 08-26 — so a rule's count is never inflated by dates when it said something else, or nothing.

“Broken & uncited” counts breaches in edits where the rule ID appears nowhere in the file: the rule was neither followed nor considered. Where the gap between the two columns is small, the agent cited the rule and broke it anyway.

Not counted as breaches. 16 edits cite R5.1 between 08-08 and 08-10, while it was a blank line with no text. A blank rule cannot be violated, so those are false citations rather than breaches, and appear in no count here.
